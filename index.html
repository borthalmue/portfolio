Robotics

FRC 9038 — SF Unity: Fabrication Lead

2025–2026 Season · Senior Year

FIRST Robotics Competition · "REBUILT" game season · San Francisco, CA

As Fabrication Lead for Team 9038 (SF Unity) during the 2025–2026 "REBUILT" season — my last season before starting at Santa Clara University — I ran the shop side of the build: taking parts from CAD to finished, robot-ready components. The biggest process change that season was bringing CNC routing/milling fully into our workflow and CAM-programming parts — including structural panels — that were previously cut or fabricated by hand.

Our 2026 robot, "Claude" — a turreted ball-shooting robot with a 40-ball capacity inside a 28"×27"×26" starting footprint, built around a swerve drivebase and a 420° turret — won Excellence in Engineering at the East Bay District Event and took 3 playoff matches across two competitive events, the best result in team history. Nearly every structural plate and panel on that robot passed through my CAM programming before it hit the CNC.

What I did

  • Led fabrication of the drivetrain, superstructure, and electronics panels from CAD models
  • Introduced and ran CNC machining for the team, replacing manual cutting on key parts
  • CAM-programmed toolpaths for structural panels and plates (feeds/speeds, tabbing, nesting)
  • Designed and CNC'd the turret mirror plate — one of the precision-fit parts I owned start to finish
  • Worked directly from CAD assemblies to translate designer intent into machinable geometry
  • Managed fabrication scheduling to keep pace with build-season deadlines

Scope note: of the parts referenced here, everything is fabricated/CAM'd/CNC'd by me except the belly pan (made by a partner shop) and purchased COTS components (bearings, fasteners, motors, etc).

Electromagnetic Radiation Gimbal Demo System

Independent build

Mechanical design · gear-driven 2-axis gimbal

A scratch-built demonstration gimbal designed to aim and stabilize a potential electromagnetic-radiation emitting device. The mount uses a geartrain to drive two independent rotation axes, letting a payload be pointed and held steady on a target point the way a focused-energy or directional-antenna system would need to be aimed.

How it works

  • Two-axis gear-driven gimbal — independent azimuth and elevation control
  • Geartrain reduces motor speed into precise, controllable angular movement
  • Designed as a mechanical testbed for aiming/stabilizing directional-emission hardware
  • Built around the same CAD-to-fabrication pipeline used in FRC work

Precisely aiming a directional energy source — whether RF, microwave, or otherwise — is a mechanical problem as much as an electrical one. This project explores the mechanism side: how to get repeatable, controllable two-axis pointing out of simple gears and motors — the same core problem behind the focused-beam concepts explored in the Masers in Medicine research, and a simpler cousin of the multi-joint targeting problem in robot-arm-sim below. The turret-tracking-sim software was written to work out this gimbal's control loop specifically.

robot-arm-sim

Python · NumPy

5-DOF arm · kinematics, RRT planning, imitation-learned control

A full digital testbed for a 5-DOF robot arm, matched to a URDF reference and built to prep for getting an actual arm (SO-101) and doing real imitation learning through LeRobot. Forward kinematics via a DH transform chain and inverse kinematics via damped least-squares — both from scratch, no Pinocchio/PyBullet. An RRT planner finds collision-free paths around a moving obstacle, and a small from-scratch neural net (plain NumPy, hand-written backprop) is trained by imitation to reproduce those paths.

What I learned debugging it

  • Plain behavior cloning looked perfect on paper (tiny validation loss) and then failed on rollout — classic compounding error from training only on the planner's own state distribution
  • Fixed the label noise first: raw RRT paths are inconsistent run to run, so shortcutting them before training made the demonstrations far more learnable
  • Fixed the distribution-shift problem with a few rounds of DAgger — rolling the policy out, asking the planner for corrections at the states it actually visits, and retraining on those

Honest result, not a cherry-picked one: ~62% of held-out tasks land within 0.6 rad of the goal, ~60% stay fully collision-free. Documented limitations and next steps are in the README.

Control logic for a 2-axis gimbal

A simulation of the tracking logic for the gear-driven gimbal above — proportional control with slew-rate limiting (modeling a real gearbox's max speed) and a deadzone so it settles instead of jittering once it locks on. Built to work out the control loop before it ever touches a motor.
